## Purpose

**Status: R&D — outputs must be reviewed by a qualified biomedical engineer
before clinical or regulatory use.**

Provides biomedical engineering analysis for FORGE projects involving
human-interface devices. Covers:

- Biocompatibility assessment (ISO 10993 series)
- Material selection for implants and wearables (cytotoxicity, corrosion,
  leachables, sterilisation compatibility)
- Mechanical requirements for implants (fatigue in physiological environment,
  fretting, stress shielding)
- Regulatory pathway identification (FDA 510(k), PMA, CE MDR)
- Sterilisation method selection (EtO, autoclave, gamma, e-beam)
- Wearable comfort and ergonomics (pressure, thermal, skin irritation)
- Biosensor interface (electrode materials, biofouling, signal quality)
- Scaffold and tissue engineering geometry requirements

---

## Preconditions

1. Device classification (implant / wearable / diagnostic / therapeutic)
2. Contact type (no contact / surface / externally communicating / implanted)
3. Duration of contact (< 24h / 24h–30 days / > 30 days per ISO 10993-1)
4. Target anatomy and physiological environment
5. Sterilisation requirement

**R&D caveat:** This skill provides preliminary guidance only. Any finding
labelled `safety_critical=true` must be escalated to a human expert before
proceeding. This skill does not replace ISO 10993 biological evaluation
conducted by a qualified test laboratory.

## Step 1 — ISO 10993-1 Biological Evaluation Plan

Classify the device using the ISO 10993-1 matrix:

```
Contact type × Duration → Required biological evaluations

Implant device (bone contact, > 30 days):
  Required tests:
    - Cytotoxicity (ISO 10993-5)
    - Sensitisation (ISO 10993-10)
    - Intracutaneous reactivity (ISO 10993-10)
    - Systemic toxicity (ISO 10993-11)
    - Subacute/subchronic toxicity (ISO 10993-11)
    - Genotoxicity (ISO 10993-3)
    - Implantation (ISO 10993-6)
    - Haemocompatibility (ISO 10993-4)
    - Chronic toxicity (ISO 10993-11)
    - Carcinogenicity (ISO 10993-3, if applicable)
```

For each required test, state status: Existing data / Required / Waivable (with rationale).

## Step 2 — Material Biocompatibility

For each material in contact with tissue or body fluids:

| Property | Requirement | Selected Material | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cytotoxicity | ISO 10993-5 pass | Ti-6Al-4V ELI | Literature supported |
| Corrosion | ASTM F2129 | Ti-6Al-4V | Grade 23 ELI standard |
| Wear debris | ISO 17853 | UHMWPE | Concern — monitor |
| Leachables | Per ISO 10993-17 | Ti-6Al-4V | Low risk metals |
| Sterilisation | EtO / autoclave | Compatible | Verified |

---

## Step 3 — Mechanical Performance in Physiological Environment

For implants:

- Fatigue life: physiological loading cycles (hip: 10⁶/year, spine: 10⁷/year)
- Fretting and galvanic corrosion at interfaces (dissimilar metals)
- Stress shielding: E_implant vs. E_bone (bone: 15–25 GPa cortical)
- Fracture toughness requirement (K_IC ≥ 50 MPa√m for metals)

Flag if implant E_modulus > 5× bone E_modulus (excessive stress shielding → bone resorption).

---

## Step 4 — Regulatory Pathway

Identify the regulatory pathway:

| Region | Route | Typical timeline | Predicate needed? |
|---|---|---|---|
| USA (FDA) | 510(k) — Class II | 3–12 months | Yes |
| USA (FDA) | PMA — Class III | 1–3 years | No |
| EU | CE MDR Class IIb | 6–18 months | Clinical evaluation |
| EU | CE MDR Class III | 12–24 months | Full clinical evidence |

State classification basis and whether a predicate device exists.

---

## Step 5 — Sterilisation Selection

| Method | Materials compatible | Log reduction | Residuals |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ethylene oxide (EtO) | Most polymers, metals | ≥ 6-log SAL 10⁻⁶ | EtO + ECH — off-gas required |
| Autoclave (121°C) | Metals, some polymers | ≥ 6-log | None |
| Gamma irradiation | Metals, limited polymers | ≥ 6-log | Polymer degradation risk |
| E-beam | Similar to gamma | ≥ 6-log | Polymer degradation risk |

Select based on material compatibility AND packaging compatibility.

## What This Skill Does NOT Do

- Does not model in vivo drug pharmacokinetics
