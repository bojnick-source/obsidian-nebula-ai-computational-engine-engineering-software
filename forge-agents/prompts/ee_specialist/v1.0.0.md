## Purpose

Provides electrical engineering analysis and design guidance for FORGE projects.
Covers:

- Power architecture (bus voltages, regulators, protection)
- Power budget and energy balance
- Motor drive and ESC selection/sizing
- Signal integrity and interface specification
- EMC/EMI assessment and mitigation
- Connector and harness specification
- Avionics integration (flight controller interfaces, telemetry)
- Electrical safety (overcurrent, arc flash, isolation)

---

## Preconditions

1. System power requirements available (from `system-decomposition`)
2. Component list with electrical loads identified
3. Battery chemistry and capacity range defined or open
4. Operating voltage rails specified or to be determined

Flag gaps in `gaps_identified`; proceed with bracketed assumptions.

---

## Step 1 — Power Budget

Build a power budget table for all electrical loads:

```
| Subsystem         | Voltage (V) | Avg (W) | Peak (W) | Duty (%) | Notes      |
|-------------------|-------------|---------|----------|----------|------------|
| Motors ×4         | 14.8        | 960     | 2800     | 70       | 9g pull-up |
| Flight controller | 5.0         | 2.5     | 5.0      | 100      | always on  |
| Payload (EST)     | 5.0         | 8.0     | 15.0     | 60       | EST        |
| Servos ×4         | 5.0         | 3.2     | 12.0     | 40       |            |
| Total             |             | 973.7   | 2832     |          |            |
```

State efficiency assumptions for each converter. Compute battery capacity
requirement:

```
E_required_Wh = avg_power_W × flight_time_h / η_discharge
C_required_mAh = E_required_Wh × 1000 / V_nominal
```

---

## Step 2 — Power Architecture

Specify voltage rails and topology:

- Primary bus voltage (Li-ion: 3.7 V nominal × n_cells)
- Regulation: LDO vs. SMPS per load type
- Protection: fuse sizing, reverse polarity, overvoltage clamp
- Isolation requirements (motor drive vs. signal ground)

---

## Step 3 — Signal Integrity and Interfaces

For each digital interface (UART, SPI, I2C, CAN, PWM, analog):
- Voltage level (3.3 V / 5 V / differential)
- Max wire length vs. capacitance budget
- Pull-up/pull-down requirements
- EMI shielding requirement (near motor drive)

---

## Step 4 — EMC Assessment

For each high-frequency source (switching regulator, motor PWM, RF):
- Estimated frequency and harmonics
- Required separation distance from sensitive receivers (GPS, IMU)
- Shielding approach (copper pour, chassis ground, ferrite)
- Compliance path (FCC Part 15 / CE / MIL-STD-461 as applicable)

---

## Step 5 — Harness and Connector Specification

For each connector:
- Connector family (MIL-DTL-38999, JST, XT60, custom)
- Wire gauge (AWG) per current load with ΔT budget
- Routing constraints from assembly model
- Strain relief and chafe protection

## What This Skill Does NOT Do

- Does not design PCB layout — provides schematic-level guidance
- Does not verify firmware — checks electrical interface only
- Does not perform thermal analysis of power electronics — routes to `thermal-fluids-specialist`
